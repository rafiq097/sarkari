# Build a Comprehensive Indian Government Exams Research Portal

Act as a senior research analyst, Indian government recruitment expert, data researcher, and senior frontend engineer.

Build a complete, beautiful, highly interactive **static website covering Indian Central Government exams and all major recruitment pathways across Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka**.

## 1. Research First — Accuracy Is the Highest Priority

Conduct extensive online research before building the website. Take as much time as necessary. Do not produce a superficial directory or invent missing information.

Cover all identifiable major recruitment organizations, examinations, recruitment routes, departments, and eligible job categories, including:

- **Central:** UPSC, SSC, Railways, banking, defence, CAPF, scientific and technical organizations, ISRO, DRDO, BARC, NIC, PSUs, GATE-based recruitment, teaching, research, regulatory bodies, EPFO, ESIC, FCI, courts, and other central recruitment organizations.
- **Andhra Pradesh:** APPSC, police, teaching, engineering, revenue, clerical, technical, departmental, and other state recruitment.
- **Telangana:** TGPSC, police, teaching, engineering, revenue, clerical, technical, departmental, and other state recruitment.
- **Karnataka:** KPSC/KAS, police, teaching, engineering, revenue, clerical, technical, departmental, and other state recruitment.

Also identify less widely known recruitment bodies and specialist exams. Do not assume that every organization uses a separate competitive examination; include direct recruitment, interviews, skill tests, physical tests, GATE-based selection, and other routes where applicable.

Use primary sources first: official recruitment portals, examination notifications, detailed advertisements, official syllabuses, government pay rules, service regulations, annual reports, official results, and official vacancy statistics. Use credible secondary sources only when primary information is unavailable, and label them accordingly.

Every important factual claim should have a source URL, source title, and relevant publication/notification year where available. Record the date on which the information was verified.

Never fabricate eligibility rules, vacancies, cutoffs, salaries, selection rates, bonuses, or promotion timelines. Distinguish confirmed facts, estimates, historical information, and unavailable data.

## 2. Historical and Current Status

Include three distinct categories:

1. **Active:** applications open or an officially announced recruitment/examination schedule exists.
2. **Recurring / expected:** an established recruitment pathway without a currently confirmed notification.
3. **Historical / inactive / discontinued:** previous examinations or recruitment pathways that must not be mistaken for current opportunities.

Use the latest available information as of the actual research date. Clearly display notification dates, application deadlines, exam dates, result dates, and the last verified date when available. Never present predicted dates as official dates.

## 3. Complete Examination and Job Profiles

Create a detailed, standardized profile for every identified examination, recruitment pathway, and important post. Include all applicable fields below, marking genuinely inapplicable or unavailable fields appropriately.

**Identity and recruitment**
- Exam/recruitment name, abbreviation, recruiting organization, department, post names, job responsibilities, employment type, and official notification links.
- Recruitment method, stages, selection process, interview requirements, skill/typing tests, physical standards, medical standards, document verification, and probation.
- Whether selection uses a national/state exam, a separate departmental exam, GATE, merit, direct recruitment, or another route.

**Eligibility**
- Minimum and maximum age, reference date for age calculation, category-wise relaxations, nationality/domicile requirements, educational qualifications, required degree/branch, minimum marks/CGPA rules, experience, final-year eligibility, attempts, physical standards, and other conditions.
- Reservation rules for applicable categories, including SC/ST/OBC-NCL/EWS/PwBD/ex-servicemen and state-specific categories where applicable.
- Explain differences between eligibility for an exam and eligibility for a particular post.
- Highlight disqualifying conditions and important exceptions.

**Exam structure and syllabus**
- Prelims, mains, papers, subjects, sections, topic-by-topic syllabus, optional subjects, descriptive papers, interviews, and qualifying stages.
- Total marks, marks per section, question count, duration, question types, language/medium options, negative marking, normalization, qualifying marks, minimum sectional scores, and weightage.
- Explain merit calculation, final score calculation, tie-breaking rules, qualifying versus merit-counting papers, and the effect of each stage on final selection.
- Include official syllabus links and distinguish the official syllabus from supplementary preparation topics.

**Cutoffs, ranks, and selection**
- Historical category-wise cutoffs, year, stage, marks scale, vacancies, applicants where officially reported, candidates shortlisted, final selections, and rank/merit-list rules.
- Explain AIR, state rank, category rank, merit rank, score, percentile, and qualifying cutoff wherever relevant.
- Explain how ranks translate into post or service allocation, including preference forms, reservation, vacancies, and counselling where applicable.
- Never invent a guaranteed rank-to-post mapping.

**Success rate, competition, difficulty, and risk**
- Calculate actual selection rates when reliable data exists: selected candidates divided by a clearly defined applicant or appeared-candidate population.
- Provide the numerator, denominator, year, source, and formula.
- Distinguish application-based selection rate from appeared-candidate selection rate.
- If reliable figures are unavailable, say so and provide a clearly labeled qualitative competition estimate instead.
- Give separate difficulty ratings for syllabus depth, competition, time pressure, technical complexity, physical requirements, and selection uncertainty.
- Use transparent low/medium/high risk assessments covering limited vacancies, irregular recruitment, changing eligibility, long selection cycles, cancellation or litigation history where documented, and uncertainty in future notifications.
- Explain that estimated difficulty and risk are analytical judgments, not official ratings. Never assign unsupported numerical success probabilities to individuals.

## 4. Salary and Full Career Breakdown

For every applicable post, research and present:

- Official pay level, pay matrix, pay scale, starting basic pay, pay band where relevant, and applicable pay commission/rules.
- Estimated gross salary and in-hand salary, with the calculation assumptions.
- DA, HRA, transport allowance, special allowances, location-dependent benefits, deductions, pension/NPS/UPS applicability where relevant, and other employment benefits.
- Annual increments, increment rules, promotion channels, grade progression, departmental examinations, MACP or equivalent progression where applicable, and realistic promotion dependencies.
- Career trajectory from entry-level post to senior positions, with typical or officially specified timelines clearly distinguished.
- Bonuses, performance incentives, festival/production bonuses, overtime, risk allowances, medical benefits, leave, housing, travel concessions, insurance, retirement benefits, and other perks only where applicable and supported by evidence.
- Posting locations, transfer frequency, rural/urban possibilities, field versus desk work, work-life balance, job stability, workload, public interaction, physical/operational hazards, and other practical trade-offs.

Do not confuse basic pay with gross or in-hand salary. Do not treat DA, bonuses, promotions, or allowances as guaranteed when they are conditional. Clearly date salary figures and explain how central and state pay rules differ.

## 5. Comparison and Decision Tools

Build useful tools that allow users to:

- Search exams and posts by name, department, organization, and keyword.
- Filter by Central/AP/Telangana/Karnataka, qualification, degree branch, age eligibility, category, salary, job type, selection stages, exam status, and technical/non-technical nature.
- Compare multiple exams side by side.
- Identify exams for which a sample candidate may be eligible, based on user-entered details.
- Compare syllabus overlap to identify opportunities for preparing for multiple exams together.
- Compare salary, difficulty, competition, recruitment frequency, career growth, location flexibility, and selection uncertainty.
- View exam timelines, application deadlines, notification histories, cutoffs, vacancies, and selection statistics where data exists.
- Bookmark exams and save comparison selections locally.
- Show a clear distinction between confirmed facts and estimates.

Eligibility tools must explicitly state that their results are indicative and that the official notification is authoritative.

## 6. Website Design and UX

Build a premium, polished research portal, not a basic static list.

Use a consistent design system, excellent typography, generous spacing, subtle gradients, refined cards, clean tables, responsive layouts, and meaningful visual hierarchy.

Include:
- A strong homepage with a search hero, category navigation, featured exams, current recruitment status, and useful statistics only when supported by data.
- Separate Central Government, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka sections.
- Exam directory, detailed exam profiles, salary and career explorer, comparison interface, eligibility checker, syllabus explorer, cutoff/selection analytics, and source directory.
- Responsive navigation, breadcrumbs, search suggestions, sorting, multi-select filters, empty states, loading-free static rendering, and helpful error/unknown-data states.
- Smooth page transitions where appropriate, scroll-triggered reveals, subtle hover effects, micro-interactions, animated counters only for real data, and tasteful motion that respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Accessible color contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic HTML, labels, focus indicators, and responsive mobile/tablet/desktop layouts.
- A professional light/dark theme toggle if it can be implemented reliably.

Avoid excessive animation, clutter, fake statistics, decorative charts without data, and unnecessary UI elements.

## 7. Static Architecture and Technology

Use a **multi-page or multi-view static website** with HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript. No backend, server-side database, or paid API is required.

Organize the project cleanly. Suggested structure:

- `index.html`
- `central/`
- `states/andhra-pradesh/`
- `states/telangana/`
- `states/karnataka/`
- `exams/` or generated static exam-detail pages
- `data/` for structured, source-attributed examination data
- `assets/css/`
- `assets/js/`
- `assets/images/`
- `sources/` or a source manifest

You may improve this architecture when justified. Keep research data separate from UI code so it can be updated without rewriting the interface.

The site must work as a deployable static site on GitHub Pages or a similar static host. Avoid dependencies on a local development server for essential functionality. If JSON fetching requires HTTP serving, document that clearly and provide a suitable local preview command. Do not build a fake backend or require secrets.

Use charts only when based on collected data. Clearly label incomplete datasets and avoid misleading comparisons between different years or selection methods.

## 8. Quality Assurance and Completion

Work in phases:

1. Inventory recruitment organizations, exams, posts, and recruitment pathways.
2. Research official sources and build a structured, source-attributed dataset.
3. Validate eligibility, syllabus, marks, pay, selection rules, and historical/current status.
4. Build the website and connect all views to the researched data.
5. Test links, filters, search, comparisons, eligibility calculations, navigation, responsive layouts, and accessibility.
6. Audit the content for unsupported claims, duplicated records, stale dates, broken links, and missing source attribution.

Create a research coverage report listing organizations examined, recruitment pathways included, source quality, data completeness, unresolved gaps, and verification dates. Report coverage honestly; do not claim literally exhaustive coverage if it has not been achieved.

Every exam must have its own useful profile, not merely a name in a directory. Prioritize completeness of information over the number of superficially listed exams.

**Final deliverable:** a fully implemented, polished static website with its complete source code, structured researched data, source references, and a README explaining how to preview, deploy, and update it. Do not stop after producing a plan, mockup, homepage, or sample exam. Continue until the functional website and research deliverables are complete.

If the work exceeds one session, maintain a progress file documenting completed research, verified sources, pending organizations, known gaps, and the next steps. Resume from that file rather than restarting or silently dropping scope.

**Non-negotiable:** factual accuracy, transparent uncertainty, traceable sources, practical career information, and working functionality take priority over visual polish alone.
